Hello to our dedicated caregiving staff

Nursing assistants and assistive personnel are the cornerstone of caring for the older adult. You are the ones who are in daily, direct, intimate, and continuous contact with our residents and patients. Your dedicated and daily work is the true protective wall that safeguards their quality of life, their personal safety, and the protection of their dignity and rights at every single moment.

You are the eyes and ears of the entire ward staff at the patient's bedside.

To provide you with the best professional tools, to strengthen your self-confidence at work, and to help you cope better and more safely with complex or unexpected situations, we are happy to launch in our institution the new hybrid training program in collaboration with the Association of Nurses for the Advancement of Geriatric Nursing and in accordance with the updated procedure of the Ministry of Health (Procedure 1.2.1.2)

We know that each and every one of you comes with a unique background, language, culture, and different professional experience. Therefore, this program was specially built to be easily accessible and tailored for you. The program combines short frontal training sessions in the ward led by our mentoring nurses, alongside easy and personal digital learning that includes short and focused instructional videos on mobile or computer, allowing you to review the principles of safety and care at the time and pace that suit you.

During the training, we will learn together and practice practical tools on critical topics for your daily work: respectful communication with the older adult, safe assistance with eating and bathing, fall prevention, maintaining skin integrity and preventing pressure ulcers, and rapid identification of distress and emergency situations and immediate reporting to a nurse.

It is important for us to emphasize: Our goal is not to criticize, blame, or punish. We work together out of a "learning culture," where open reporting is our only way to learn, to protect you and the patients, and to prevent errors or difficulties from "Near Miss" incidents and risks before they happen.

Upon completion of the frontal sessions and digital learning, each and every one of you will be awarded an official training certificate signed by the Director of Nursing. This certificate, which will be proudly filed in your personal file, reflects your commitment to high professionalism and your full partnership in leading the quality of care in the ward.

You are the beating heart of our institution, and your contribution to the group and the residents is immense and irreplaceable. I invite you all to take an active part in this important training, to grow professionally, and to continue doing your sacred work with confidence and great pride.

◆ The 360 Quality and Safety Kit includes:

We have developed and implemented a thoughtful pilot based on five synchronizing tools that support each other and provide a complete solution for the ward:

1. Clinical Safety and Knowledge Control Guide: An accessible booklet in the staff's language detailing day-to-day (ADL) procedures for preventing aspiration during eating, burns during bathing, falls during transfers, and severe clinical prohibitions.

2. Lesson Plan and Management Guide: Targeted 15-minute nurse training to establish clinical leadership in a multicultural environment and dismantle the fear of authority.

3. Weekly Observation Sheet (Audit Checklist): A quick 2-minute field control tool for checking the actual performance of caregivers and providing constructive feedback in the spirit of the ADKAR model.

4. 'Near Miss' Board and Poster: An anonymous physical board in the staff room to encourage safe reporting and group learning, without fear and punishment.

5. Smart Excel Tracking Log: A computerized system that automatically calculates in real-time the competence percentages and night competence according to the requirements of section 6.4 of the procedure.

Critical Emphases for Multicultural Communication and Clinical Leadership

As clinical mentors leading a culturally diverse team, you must pay attention to the following emphases:

1. Dismantling authority barriers: In certain cultures, directly addressing a senior nurse to report a difficulty or mistake is perceived as an affront to professional dignity or a threat to the workplace. Use constructive and blame-neutralizing feedback to earn their trust.

2. Bridging language gaps in emergencies: Practice with the staff the use of simple and precise keywords when calling for help and avoid interrupting the caregiver's words during reporting. Ask closed and simple "yes/no" questions to make it easier for them.

3. Accessible visual solutions: Prefer the use of colored symbols, icons, and magnets (such as a red mark above the bed) that make the safety message quickly accessible to all team members regardless of their level of Hebrew.

1. Safety and Risk Prevention Guide for Assistive Personnel in Geriatrics

Training and Leadership in a Multicultural Environment

The caregiving and nursing staff in our institution is composed of an amazing and diverse human mosaic of different cultures, languages, and backgrounds. We see this diversity as a source of ward strength and resilience. Clinical leadership means ensuring "cultural alignment" and maintaining a psychologically safe work environment, where every caregiver feels comfortable expressing difficulty, asking questions, and reporting mistakes without fear. We are committed to making all training materials accessible in a simple, accessible, and respectful linguistic and visual manner for everyone.

The Registered Nurse as the Leader of Safety - The Patient's Protective Wall

The work of the caregiver (assistive personnel) is carried out under the guidance and supervision of a registered nurse who bears the professional and managerial responsibility for the caregivers' work. This guide is intended to assist you in maintaining care safety and preventing risks around the clock.

◆ Part A: Maintaining Safety and Preventing Risks in Day-to-Day Routine (ADL)

🍽️ Assistance with Eating and Drinking (Section 5.1.7) - Prevention of Aspiration

  • Mandatory full seating at 90 degrees during the meal and for at least 30 minutes afterwards to prevent inhalation of food into the lungs (aspiration).
  • Strict adherence only to the approved food and drink textures for each resident (according to professional guidelines).
  • Immediate cessation of feeding and urgent reporting to the nurse in any case of coughing, choking, or the appearance of a wet/bubbling voice during food intake.

🛀 Assistance with Bathing and Hygiene (Section 5.1.2) - Prevention of Burns and Wounds

  • Mandatory physical check of the water temperature on the back of the caregiver's hand before starting the bath and before water contact with the patient's skin to prevent burns.
  • Maintaining skin integrity through gentle wiping of skin folds, thorough drying, and immediate reporting to the nurse of initial redness (an early and life-saving sign of pressure ulcers).
  • Insisting on anti-slip mats and ensuring the wheels of the shower chair are locked.

🚶 Safe Mobility and Transfers (Section 5.1.4) - Prevention of Falls

  • Performing transfers (from bed to chair and back) only according to the professional guidelines set by the nurse (single, in pairs, or using a designated hoist).
  • Complete and secure locking of the wheels of the bed or wheelchair before starting the transfer.
  • Ensuring the resident is wearing closed, supportive, and non-slip shoes before standing up. Absolute prohibition on leaving a high-risk resident unattended in the bathroom or toilet.

⚠ Part B: Boundaries of Sector and Prohibited Actions without a Nurse (Procedure 1.2.1.2)

  • According to the Ministry of Health Procedure 1.2.1.2, the registered nurse bears the exclusive professional and managerial responsibility for the caregivers' work. The division of areas of activity is clear and intended to prevent severe clinical risks.
  • ⚠ Prohibition of independent decision-making: The caregiver is not authorized to make therapeutic or clinical decisions independently under any circumstances. All their activities are subject to the nurse's instructions, and any change in care or deviation from the routine must receive the nurse's prior approval.
  • ⚠ Prohibition of engaging in advanced skills: There is an absolute prohibition on the caregiver performing invasive procedures or advanced clinical skills of a nurse, such as distributing medications, performing dressings, tube feeding, testing blood sugar levels, or administering insulin.
  • ⚠ Critical prohibitions in medical emergencies (Appendix B in Procedure 1.2.1.2):
  • In suspicion of a cerebrovascular event (stroke): Absolute prohibition to give the resident food or drink due to the immediate risk of choking (severe dysphagia)!
  • During seizures and convulsions: Absolute prohibition to insert objects into the resident's mouth or attempt to physically restrain body movements.
  • Upon identifying signs of hypoglycemia: Absolute prohibition to give the resident food or a sweet drink until a blood sugar test is performed by the registered nurse and her official approval is given!

⚡ Part C: Identifying 6 Emergency Situations at Night (Appendix B of the Procedure)

Emergency Situation and Permitted Treatment (Assistive Personnel) Immediate Report to the Registered Nurse Clinical Symptoms for Quick Identification
Situation 1: Extreme Breathing Difficulties
Permitted treatment: Immediate seating in a semi-sitting position (tilting the bed), loosening tight clothing, calming and supporting with gentle speech, verifying the airway is clear.		 Call the nurse immediately and report:
'Mr./Mrs. [Name] is suffering from severe and urgent breathing difficulties.'
Stay physically next to the resident until she arrives.		 ⚡ Severe breathing difficulties:
Resident struggles to breathe, rapid and shallow breathing, loud wheezing or rattling sounds, use of accessory muscles (retractions), bluish color around the lips or nails, restlessness, confusion, and extreme and sudden anxiety.
Situation 2: Suspicion of a Cerebrovascular Event (Stroke)
Permitted treatment: Laying the resident on their back with the head slightly elevated (30 degrees), protection against falling. It is forbidden to give food or drink under any circumstances!		 Call the nurse immediately and report:
'Mr./Mrs. [Name] has sudden difficulty speaking or difficulty closing the mouth and limb weakness.'
Stay with the resident.		 ⚡ Suspicion of a stroke:
Distortion or drooping of one side of the face (crooked smile), sudden weakness in an arm or leg (arm drop), difficulty speaking or severe confusion. Speech is garbled and unclear, sudden excessive sleepiness or loss of consciousness.
Situation 3: Chest Pains / Heart Attack
Permitted treatment: Immediate seating in a semi-sitting position, calming and reducing anxiety, quick call for a nurse's help, and bringing the resuscitation cart and defibrillator close to the resident's room.		 Call the nurse immediately and report:
'Mr./Mrs. [Name] is complaining of chest pains, with restlessness and abnormal breathing.'		 ⚡ Chest pains (Heart):
Complaint of heavy pressure or strong pain in the center of the chest, pain radiating to the shoulder, arm, or jaw, accompanying shortness of breath, cold sweat, and pallor. The resident is frightened and holds their chest, extreme restlessness, sudden rattling.
Situation 4: Seizures and Convulsions
Permitted treatment: Laying on the side to prevent choking on secretions, protecting the head from injury using a pillow or soft clothing, removing sharp objects. Prohibition of physical restraint or inserting objects into the mouth!		 Call the nurse immediately and report:
'Mr./Mrs. (Name) is convulsing and unconscious.'
Monitor the resident completely until she arrives.		 ⚡ Seizures and convulsions:
Sudden loss of consciousness, muscle rigidity, and violent uncontrollable shaking all over the body, eye rolling, foam from the mouth or tongue biting, loss of sphincter control.
Situation 5: Hypoglycemia (Drop in Blood Sugar)
Permitted treatment: Maintaining a safe position in bed or a chair to prevent falls and injury, urgent call to a nurse. Absolute prohibition on giving sugar or food without a nurse's approval and testing!		 Call the nurse immediately and report:
'Mr./Mrs. (Name) is sweating, trembling, and confused - sudden signs of a drop in blood sugar (suspicion of hypoglycemia).' Stay close to the resident.		 ⚡ Blood sugar drop (Hypoglycemia):
Symptoms appearing rapidly: intense feeling of hunger, cold sweat, strong tremor in the limbs, palpitations, confusion, slurred speech, dizziness, extreme weakness or anxiety, and a rapid change in the state of consciousness.
Situation 6: Severe External Bleeding
Permitted treatment: Mandatory use of gloves for self-protection, strong direct pressure on the source of the bleeding with clean gauze or a towel. Elevating the bleeding limb if possible (if there is no fracture).		 Call the nurse immediately and report:
'Mr./Mrs. (Name) has massive external bleeding.'
Continue to press on the bleeding site continuously.		 ⚡ Massive and severe bleeding:
Large and rapidly expanding hemorrhage, bedding or clothes soaked in a lot of blood, extreme pallor, rapid pulse, and cold sweat of the resident.

🗣️ Overcoming the Language and Authority Barrier in Emergency Situations

In stress and emergency situations, language difficulties can intensify. Make sure to use the bold red keywords in the table. The nurse receiving the report is trained to listen patiently, not to interrupt your words, and to ask simple, closed questions to quickly verify the medical condition. The safety of the resident and the quick call for help are the only things that matter - not perfect grammar!

📊 Part D: Regulatory Response to the Duty of Documentation, Control, and Continuous Evaluation

The Ministry of Health Procedure 1.2.1.2 establishes strict control and documentation duties on the medical institution.

Below is the manner in which the hybrid training program closes the regulatory response in your ward:

Detailed Documentation Duty (Section 6.6.4) 📝

Every training session, frontal or digital, is documented in the system in a computerized and orderly manner: dates of execution, exact content learned, names of participating caregivers, their signatures, and the instructor's signature. The hybrid training program of the Nurses' Association provides a structured and complete digital record of learning progress, actual training scope, and its participants, serving as solid proof before inspection bodies during Ministry of Health audits.

Continuous Digital Knowledge Control (Section 6.6.3) 📝

Since the procedure allows conducting up to half of the theoretical training via digital means, the digital learning includes short instructional videos illustrating field situations. At the end of each digital unit, a mandatory knowledge quiz is held, allowing assessment of the caregiver's understanding and identifying weak topics requiring the mentoring nurse to provide complementary training or practical practice in the ward.

Semi-Annual Continuous Performance Evaluation (Section 6.6.6) 📝

Nursing management and nurses in charge are obligated to perform a documented continuous performance evaluation in the employee's file at least once every half year. This evaluation is carried out through periodic observations and constructive feedback conversations, backed up by the ward's digital tracking log.

Official and Documented Certificate of Completion: 📝

Upon completion of the frontal training, digital learning, and knowledge quizzes, the caregiver receives an official training certificate signed by the Director of Nursing at the institution. The certificate is filed in the employee's file and reflects their commitment to learning and professional competence.

📝 Part E: ADKAR Model Checklist for the Senior Nurse for Implementing Safety Procedures

❑ A - Awareness: Explain to the assistive personnel the 'why' behind the procedures and their importance. Translate the dry metrics into clinical practice: Show them how identifying nutritional decline or skin redness prevents severe hospitalizations and preserves the resident's dignity.

❑ D - Desire: Neutralize the fear of reporting. Clarify to them that reporting 'near misses' or minor mistakes is a tool for protected ward learning and not a ground for punishment. Share practical solutions with them (like a visual marking of residents at risk) to strengthen their sense of partnership and control.

❑ K - Knowledge: Integrate the association's frontal training together with short digital instructional videos. Ensure that the assistive personnel understand exactly the division of roles: what are the permitted actions for them (ADL, safe mobility, position changes) and what are the prohibited actions that must be performed only by a registered nurse.

❑ A - Ability: Conduct simulations and practical exercises during shifts. Ensure that during busy times there is physical backup in the field allowing caregivers to perform transfers and mobility in pairs according to guidelines, without dangerous shortcuts.

❑ R - Reinforcement: Perform ongoing observations and documented periodic evaluations (at least once every half year in the employee's file). Give public praise and compliments to caregivers excelling in quick reporting and alertness to changes in residents' skin, and celebrate ward successes of a decrease in falls or pressure ulcers.

Detailed Clinical Answers and Explanations Sheet

This page is intended for the nurse in charge / director of nursing conducting the training, for the purpose of providing procedure-based feedback, in-depth group discussion, and reinforcing the care safety culture in the ward.

Question 1: The correct answer is B.

Clinical and managerial explanation (Procedure 1.2.1.2 - Appendix B): Symptoms of cold sweat, tremor, extreme weakness, and confusion raise a suspicion of hypoglycemia (drop in blood sugar). The procedure explicitly states that there is an absolute prohibition for assistive personnel to give the patient food or drink (including sweet drink) until a blood sugar test is performed by a registered nurse, in order to prevent a risk of aspiration in a state of confusion or semi-consciousness, and also for a precise and safe diagnosis of the situation.

Question 2: The correct answer is B.

Clinical and managerial explanation (Procedure 1.2.1.2 - Section 5.1.7): Sudden coughing, choking, or the appearance of a wet/bubbling voice during eating are clear warning signs of aspiration (penetration of food or liquid into the trachea instead of the esophagus). In such a situation, it is mandatory to stop feeding immediately and prevent drinking, leave the patient in an upright sitting position, and urgently call a nurse. Aspiration of food into the lungs poses an immediate life-threatening risk to a frail resident and can cause severe pneumonia and urgent hospitalization.

Question 3: The correct answer is C.

Clinical and managerial explanation (Procedure 1.2.1.2 - Appendix B): Identification of a drooping face or speech difficulty raises an immediate suspicion of a brain stroke (cerebrovascular event). The initial treatment permitted for the caregiver is laying the resident on his back with his head slightly elevated (to reduce intracranial pressure), full protection from falling and trauma, and calm speech. There is an absolute prohibition to give the resident water or food due to a severe and frequent impairment in the swallowing mechanism (dysphagia) during a stroke, which puts him at risk of immediate aspiration and choking.

Question 4: The correct answer is C.

Clinical and managerial explanation (Procedure 1.2.1.2 - Appendix B): During convulsions, maintaining the airway is critical. The resident must be laid on his side to allow secretions to drain out and prevent choking. It is mandatory to remove sharp or hard objects to protect him from physical injury. The procedure explicitly forbids assistive personnel from inserting objects or fingers into the resident's mouth (fear of choking or severe injury) and also forbids attempting to physically restrain his body movements (could cause fractures or joint dislocations).

Question 5: The correct answer is C.

Clinical and managerial explanation (Procedure 1.2.1.2 - Section 5.1.2): The skin of frail geriatric patients is thin, delicate, and has reduced sensitivity to heat and cold. Checking the water on the back of the caregiver's hand is the most effective active protective layer to prevent severe burns. Relying solely on a temperature regulator or the resident's input is insufficient, as systemic control mechanisms or sensory perceptions may be misleading.

2. Lesson Plan and Management Guide for Nurses in Charge

Safety Management, Supervision, and Leadership of Assistive Personnel in a Quarter-Hour per Week (Procedure 1.2.1.2 updated 12-2025)

Chapter 1: Why Are We Here? The Duty of Regulatory Supervision and Tracking [2 minutes]

The Ministry of Health's Geriatrics Division Procedure 1.2.1.2 (dated 18/12/2025) stipulates that the nurse in charge holds the professional and managerial authority and responsibility for the caregivers' work (assistive personnel) in the ward. The procedure requires maintaining close and organized supervision and control around the clock (Section 4.3). During the night shift, under the framework that allows supervision by a single registered nurse over two wards (with 3 caregivers), the caregivers' competence for the rapid identification of 6 medical emergencies (respiratory, stroke, chest pain, convulsions, hypoglycemia, and massive bleeding) is a critical and life-saving condition (Appendix B).

The hybrid program of the Nurses' Association provides the nurse with the tools to act as a clinical mentor leading the implementation of the procedure, and provides a structured solution to the documentation requirements for the inspection bodies.

Chapter 2: Managing the Digital Ward Tracking Log (Excel Tracker) [5 minutes]

The procedure requires documenting in every caregiver's employee file all the training they have undergone (including dates, contents, caregiver's signature, and instructor's signature), and performing a documented continuous performance evaluation at least once every half year (Section 6.6.6). To this end, we have built for you the new ward Excel Tracker - an automated log that calculates in real-time the night competence of the team (safety-training-tracker-v2.xlsx). The completion percentage (Column P) validates the basic 18 hours of training, and the 4 hours of ward BLS according to the threshold conditions of Section 6.4 of the procedure (over 3 months seniority, CPR, designated night training). Your role as nurses in charge is to update the light blue cells upon receiving a new employee or completing training, and to ensure that the data is exported and printed for filing in the employee file ahead of a Ministry of Health audit.

Chapter 3: Operating and Guiding the 'Near Miss' Board in the Staff Room [5 minutes]

The findings of the National Patient Safety Culture Survey (2020) show that the non-punitive approach to errors received the lowest score in the geriatric system (only 46% positive). Assistive personnel report severe fear of documenting mistakes in their personal files. To close the reporting gap, it is mandatory to set up the 'anonymous near miss board in the staff room, according to the designed poster (near-miss-poster.pdf). Guide the staff to write on short sticky notes, without names of employees or patients. Once a week, dedicate only 5 minutes during shift handover to present one selected note, discuss it openly and non-judgmentally, and translate it into a preventive ward directive ("safety flash") (such as hanging a safety sign above a resident at risk's bed).

Chapter 4: Using the Safety Guide for Field Training and Quick Simulations [3 minutes]

The safety guide we designed (safety-brochure-assistive-personnel-v5.pdf) serves you as a central tool for short bedside training (Bedside Teaching). Dedicate quick 2-minute field training sessions each time on day-to-day routine topics (ADL): During eating - demonstrate the 90-degree angle to prevent aspiration; During bathing - refresh the checking of water heat on the back of the hand and identifying initial redness to prevent pressure ulcers; And during transfer - practice locking wheels and ensuring closed shoes for the resident. Perform a quick weekly quality check using the observation sheet (safety-audit-checklist.pdf) managed via the ADKAR feedback model, when the score rating and completion of the assessments sync directly into the digital tracking log.

👥 Target Audience: Ward nurses in charge (clinical mentors and staff escorts)

⏱️ Ward Training Duration: 15 focused weekly minutes

🎯 Ultimate Goal: Establishing quality clinical leadership, engaging assistive personnel teams in a non-blaming learning culture, and closing the reporting gap in the field.

4. Near Miss Board: Learning Together!

Here we maintain the safety of the patients and the staff in the ward

What is a "Near Miss"? (Near Miss)

A malfunction, risk, or error discovered in time and prevented at the last moment, without the patient being harmed. We report them to fix physical hazards, change procedures, and prevent the next disaster!

◆ The Three Golden Rules of Our Board ◆

1. Full and Guaranteed Anonymity!

There is an absolute prohibition on writing names of staff members or patients on the notes. Use only room numbers or a general description of the risk. The board is intended solely for learning, not for blaming!

2. No Impact on Personal File - Zero Punishment!

Findings from the national survey show that the fear of punishment prevents reporting. Here we establish a safe culture: mistakes and near-misses are not held against you in your personal file and will not be used against you in any way.

3. Reporting Today - Preventing Tomorrow's Disaster!

Your reports are the patients' protective wall. One small note about a physical hazard or a near-miss helps the nurse in charge fix the risk and refresh the procedures before someone really gets hurt.

So How Do We Use the Board in 3 Simple Steps?

◆ Step 1: Write a small note: Did you identify a hazard or a 'near miss'? Write a short description on a colored note (e.g., 'water on the shower floor in room 4' or 'patient at risk of falling got up').

◆ Step 2: Drop it in or stick it: Stick the note in the first column of the board ('What almost happened?') or drop it completely anonymously into the red reporting box below the board.

◆ Step 3: Changing the face of the ward: The nurse in charge will collect the notes and raise one of them for a short 5-minute discussion at the shift handover to find permanent ward solutions together.

Your Partnership is Our Strength to Save Lives!

Based on Ministry of Health Procedure 1.2.1.2 and the 2020 Organizational Patient Safety Culture Survey
